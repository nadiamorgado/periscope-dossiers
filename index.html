This dossier documents the first biweekly alignment between Atomsix's PM, Development, and QA teams embedded on the Periscope project. Its purpose is to create a structured, recurring space for the Atomsix team to surface process barriers, share day-to-day perceptions, and collectively identify improvements. The goal of this cadence is not to assign blame, but to move forward together with Periscope — aligned, informed, and with a shared understanding of what needs to improve.

Development

Sprint ceremony structure Observation
  • Sprint Planning and Sprint Demo are held in the same session, making it hard to distinguish commitments from showcases.
  • Team will pay closer attention to better navigate both agendas going forward.
Carry-over tickets from previous sprints Observation
  • Some open items trace back to Sprint 5 and have not yet been resolved.
  • There are early signs of older tickets being prioritized before new work is added.
Frontend and backend pipelines out of sync Observation
  • Frontend tickets are frequently near-complete but stalled due to missing backend endpoints or fixes.
  • Sprint metrics count these as undelivered, even when the frontend work is done.
  • Suggestion: split minimal backend dependencies into their own tickets instead of holding the full frontend item open.
Ticket ownership when a blocker shifts domains Suggestion
  • When a frontend ticket is fully blocked by a backend issue, it currently stays assigned to the frontend engineer.
  • Proposal: co-assign or reassign the blocking item to the relevant backend engineer so accountability matches who needs to act.
  • Briefly discussed with the PO — received as directionally sensible, with consideration around minimizing handoff noise.
Ticket writing quality has improved Positive
  • Dedicated PO involvement in ticket creation has made descriptions more precise and complete.
  • Reduces back-and-forth and rework — one incomplete ticket was flagged, but the overall trend is positive.
AI-assisted workflows gaining traction Positive
  • A developer is exploring connecting Claude to Figma and Storybook for design and component validation.

Quality Assurance

Blocking tickets lack timely assignment Observation
  • Tickets ready for QA testing are sometimes blocked by unresolved items that haven't been assigned to any backend engineer.
  • This creates invisible delays — the dependency sits in the frontend queue with no clear owner on the other side.
QA automation on strategic hold Context
  • The INT environment receives constant developer updates, making it unsuitable for reliable automation runs.
  • Automation should resume only once a stable Staging environment is in place — a deliberate pause, not a deprioritization.
Roles & permissions causing test instability Active Blocker
  • Menu items disappear or routes become inaccessible depending on the user role in use.
  • Tests are failing due to missing permissions rather than actual functional bugs.
  • Team is refining the process to tag these as backend-origin issues to avoid misattribution.
Manual QA capacity under pressure Observation
  • With automation paused, manual testing workload has grown.
  • Suggestion: team members currently focused on automation could contribute to manual testing in the interim.
Sprint reports should reflect blocker origins Suggestion
  • End-of-sprint QA reports shared with stakeholders don't always indicate why a ticket is blocked.
  • Adding a brief root-cause note to blocked items would prevent delivery figures from being misread as frontend underperformance.
Blocker labeling and bug tickets improving Positive
  • Team is more consistently moving blocked items to "Blockage" status and opening explicit backend bug tickets.
  • Delays are becoming more traceable and the source of blockers is increasingly visible in the board.
AI-assisted QA workflows gaining traction Positive
  • One QA engineer is using a Claude integration with Jira to read ticket context before running tests — quickly surfacing whether issues are permission-related.
